# Proto-Minitongue

A constructed language developed iteratively between reconstructed proto forms and contemporary structure.

## Typological profile

Proto-Minitongue is a predominantly suffixing, SOV language with layered morphophonology and an active–stative alignment system. Intransitive subjects may pattern with either agentive/ergative or patientive/absolutive arguments according to lexical class and contrasts such as control, active involvement, and affectedness. Core and oblique relations are expressed through a productive case system, while verbal indexing marks person but not number. Recoverable indexed arguments may be omitted.

Nominal morphology has a shared number–case architecture with four inherited stem classes: **ANIMATE**, **GROWING**, **LAND/WATER**, and **MADE**. The classes are semantically motivated but partly opaque through historical erosion and analogy; they condition limited surface case allomorphy rather than separate declensional paradigms. Grammatical animacy is binary, while culturally salient **personhood** is a distinct, relational discourse category that can affect reference and person-sensitive DAT/COM morphology without changing lexical noun class. A newer, semantically richer attributive classifier system cross-cuts the inherited classes.

The verbal word combines relational phase prefixes with person indexing, root-grade morphology, derivation, applicatives, and tense–aspect suffixes. Positive, negative, and neutral **phase** interact with verb class and case relations to express contrasts such as connection/separation, entry/exit, joining/leaving, and entering or leaving states. Two applicatives promote affected persons and means/instruments. Adjectival meanings are expressed by stative verbs.

Phonologically, the core inventory is small: /p t k m n h s r j/ and /a e i u/, with marginal derived /o/. The ordinary syllable is `(C)(j)V(C)`; /ai au/ are diphthongs. Prosody is moraic and quantity-sensitive, with stress attracted to the rightmost heavy syllable within the final three syllables. Historical vowel contraction, resyllabification, coda shortening, fusion, and linker/theme vowels create substantial surface alternation while preserving a relatively compact underlying morphology.

The language is being developed diachronically: synchronic patterns are expected to have plausible Pre-Proto sources in sound change, analogy, reanalysis, grammaticalization, and lexical retention rather than being added as isolated typological features.
