# Proto-Minitongue History

This file records the language's in-world diachrony, not repository-development history.

Use it for reconstructed stages, ordered sound changes, analogy, reanalysis, grammaticalization, semantic change, lexical replacement, contact effects, and derivations needed to explain contemporary forms.

## Stages

Proto-Minitongue has one reconstructable ancestral stage, **Pre-Proto-Minitongue**, immediately preceding canonical Proto-Minitongue. Additional earlier stages are not currently posited.

## Changes

Proto-Minitongue's phonotactics and morphophonology result in part from historical repair across morpheme boundaries rather than from wholly invariant inherited syllable shapes. The following outcomes and their required relative ordering are canonical.

### Vowel contact

Pre-Proto vowel contact developed asymmetrically:

| Input | Proto-Minitongue outcome |
| --- | --- |
| `aa ee ii uu` | heavy /a e i u/ |
| `ae ai` | /ai/ |
| `au` | /au/ |
| `ei` | heavy /e/ |
| `ia ie iu` | /ja je ju/ |
| `ea eu` | /ja ju/ |
| `ua ui` | hiatus /u.a u.i/ |
| `ue` | hiatus /u.i/ |

Identical-vowel contraction preserves two morae. The `ei` merger likewise yields bimoraic /e/, but its historical source remains morphophonologically recoverable. The resulting /j/ may form the second member of a `Cj` onset. Once a nucleus is bimoraic, it is phonologically saturated: later contact with a vowel-initial morpheme does not trigger a second coalescence, and hiatus is preserved. Thus forms such as `pâ-e` and `pai-e` remain /pâ.e/ and /pai.e/ rather than being recursively contracted.

### Consonant boundaries

Assimilation and fusion precede later repair where they can resolve a boundary. Identical consonants form heterosyllabic geminates when the first member is a legal coda; onset-only /h j/ do not form such geminates. Nasal place assimilation yields phonetic [m] before /p m/ and [ŋ] before /k/ without establishing new inherited phonemes.

The inherited stops /p t k/ remain stable intervocalically at the Proto-Minitongue stage; productive intervocalic lenition has not yet developed.

### Syllabification and coda shortening

Resyllabification precedes coda-conditioned shortening. Proto-Minitongue does not permit superheavy 3μ syllables, so a bimoraic nucleus that remains before a moraic coda is reduced to a monomoraic nucleus while the lost vowel continues to condition vowel quality:

| Heavy source + coda | Proto-Minitongue |
| --- | --- |
| `aaC` | /aC/ |
| `eeC` | /eC/ |
| `iiC` | /iC/ |
| `uuC` | /uC/ |
| `aeC, aiC` | /eC/ |
| `auC` | /oC/ |
| `eiC` | /iC/ |

Thus heavy /e/ from `ee` gives /eC/, while phonologically equal heavy /e/ from `ei` gives /iC/. The /o/ produced by `auC` is a marginal derived vowel rather than part of the inherited four-vowel core. If the consonant resyllabifies as the onset of a following syllable, shortening does not apply.

### Case morphology

The case paradigm reflects several historical layers rather than a single uniform source. GEN `-n` and DAT `-r` continue old nominal relational elements, while ERG `-k` and ESS `-e` have independent origins.

The locative series was reorganized around an older `*t` relational base plus fossilized deictic material. Proto-Minitongue preserves LOC `-t` as the least specified member, INESS `-ti` for interior relation, and SUPER `-ta` for surface/support. The three endings are synchronically indivisible but remain analogically recognizable as a `t(V)` family.

PERL, INS, and COM descend from an older broad WITH/BY/THROUGH relation organized around `*m`. PERL and INS normally converge as `-m`, COM preserves `-ma`, and conservative PERL `-mi` remains productive with pronouns and a lexical set of canonical route/path nouns. The modern `m(V)` forms are indivisible case endings, but speakers recognize their relationship. Semantic and animacy-based specialization helps maintain the split: inanimate `-m` favors path, means, or functional association, while `-ma` favors companion/co-participant construal.

Ordinary nominal ABS is zero. Pronouns preserve person-specific ABS stem alternations and additional conservative core-case relics from an older pronominal paradigm; their exact forms remain to be reconstructed.

The modern pre-case /i/ linker is historically the ANIMATE class theme generalized beyond its original lexical class. The older noun-class system was semantically motivated and classifier-like, then became stem-integrated and partly opaque. An older SOCIAL class merged productively with MOBILE-LIVING to yield ANIMATE, while residual SOCIAL morphology survived separately in person-sensitive DAT and COM. Its fuller marker is reconstructable only schematically as `*Ci`; the consonant remains UNSPECIFIED.

The surviving productive theme profiles are ANIMATE `*-i`, GROWING `*-a`, LAND/WATER `*-u`, and MADE `*Ø/C`. Semantic reassignment and sound change both contributed to the collapse of older classes, while high-frequency nouns retained fossil material from extinct distinctions. Apocope obscured class marking in many ABS.SG citation forms.

Analogy spread ANIMATE `*-i` as the default linker before consonantal cases, but conservative class pockets resisted leveling. GROWING retains `*-a` especially before LOC `*-t` and PERL/INS `*-m`, yielding transparent Proto-Minitongue `-at` and `-am`. LAND/WATER analogously preserves `*-u` in `-ut` and `-um`, with additional older `u ~ Ø` and consonantal relics surviving lexically. MADE was remodeled most strongly toward the /i/ default, though older artifact nouns may retain compact direct/fused `-m` PERL/INS, reinforced by frequent instrumental uses. ANIMATE's original class status is recoverable chiefly from irregular high-frequency fusions and from later number morphology rather than from a fully separate modern declension.

Dual `-e` and plural `-u` descend from class-independent number morphology. Contact with class themes creates class-sensitive surface outputs entirely through the regular vowel-contact and weight rules; no separate class-specific number allomorphs or analogical plural leveling are required. Regular sound change can therefore create number syncretism in case-marked forms.

A bounded GROUP/PORTION formative developed as nominal collective/paucal `-s`. It creates a derived bounded subset before ordinary number inflection and, by intervening between the lexical class theme and case, blocks the conservative class-theme case pockets; generalized `-i-` is used before consonantal case endings on these derived stems. Historically related but synchronically separate developments from the same boundedness source are expected in a GEN-based partitive construction of the shape `WHOLE-GEN + PART` and in verbal morphology expressing bounded event extent, completion where lexically appropriate, or a limited series of repetitions. The exact PART reflex and verbal cognate exponent remain unreconstructed.

A later attributive classifier layer developed partly from inherited class material and partly from newer semantic classifiers. Unlike lexical noun class, it remained sensitive to current construal and could cross-cut inherited declension, allowing, for example, a LAND/WATER noun to take SOCIAL/PERSON classification in ritual discourse without changing its lexical case behavior.

### Final repair and stress

After assimilation/fusion and resyllabification, remaining illegal structures undergo restricted deletion and then /i/-epenthesis where protected lexical or morphological material would otherwise be lost. Stress is assigned only after these processes.

The resulting quantity-sensitive stress system selects the rightmost heavy syllable within the final three syllables; if that window contains no heavy syllable, stress is penultimate. All closed syllables are heavy, as are diphthongs and contracted bimoraic monophthongs.

The absolute chronology of changes that do not interact is not independently asserted; the relative ordering above is sufficient to derive the canonical Proto-Minitongue outputs.
