# Proto-Minitongue History

This file records the language's in-world diachrony, not repository-development history.

Use it for reconstructed stages, ordered sound changes, analogy, reanalysis, grammaticalization, semantic change, lexical replacement, contact effects, and derivations needed to explain contemporary forms.

## Stages

Proto-Minitongue has one reconstructable ancestral stage, **Pre-Proto-Minitongue**, immediately preceding canonical Proto-Minitongue. Additional earlier stages are not currently posited.

## Changes

Proto-Minitongue's phonotactics and morphophonology result in part from historical repair across morpheme boundaries rather than from wholly invariant inherited syllable shapes. The following outcomes and their required relative ordering are canonical.

### Vowel contact

Pre-Proto vowel contact developed asymmetrically:

| Input | Proto-Minitongue outcome |
| --- | --- |
| `aa ee ii uu` | heavy /a e i u/ |
| `ae ai` | /ai/ |
| `au` | /au/ |
| `ei` | heavy /e/ |
| `ia ie iu` | /ja je ju/ |
| `ea eu` | /ja ju/ |
| `ua ui` | hiatus /u.a u.i/ |
| `ue` | hiatus /u.i/ |

Identical-vowel contraction preserves two morae. The `ei` merger likewise yields bimoraic /e/, but its historical source remains morphophonologically recoverable. The resulting /j/ may form the second member of a `Cj` onset. Once a nucleus is bimoraic, it is phonologically saturated: later contact with a vowel-initial morpheme does not trigger a second coalescence, and hiatus is preserved. Thus forms such as `pâ-e` and `pai-e` remain /pâ.e/ and /pai.e/ rather than being recursively contracted.

### Consonant boundaries

Assimilation and fusion precede later repair where they can resolve a boundary. Identical consonants form heterosyllabic geminates when the first member is a legal coda; onset-only /h j/ do not form such geminates. Nasal place assimilation yields phonetic [m] before /p m/ and [ŋ] before /k/ without establishing new inherited phonemes.

The inherited stops /p t k/ remain stable intervocalically at the Proto-Minitongue stage; productive intervocalic lenition has not yet developed.

### Syllabification and coda shortening

Resyllabification precedes coda-conditioned shortening. Proto-Minitongue does not permit superheavy 3μ syllables, so a bimoraic nucleus that remains before a moraic coda is reduced to a monomoraic nucleus while the lost vowel continues to condition vowel quality:

| Heavy source + coda | Proto-Minitongue |
| --- | --- |
| `aaC` | /aC/ |
| `eeC` | /eC/ |
| `iiC` | /iC/ |
| `uuC` | /uC/ |
| `aeC, aiC` | /eC/ |
| `auC` | /oC/ |
| `eiC` | /iC/ |

Thus heavy /e/ from `ee` gives /eC/, while phonologically equal heavy /e/ from `ei` gives /iC/. The /o/ produced by `auC` is a marginal derived vowel rather than part of the inherited four-vowel core. If the consonant resyllabifies as the onset of a following syllable, shortening does not apply.

### Case morphology

The case paradigm reflects several historical layers rather than a single uniform source. GEN `-n` and DAT `-r` continue old nominal relational elements, while ERG `-k` and ESS `-e` have independent origins.

The locative series was reorganized around an older `*t` relational base plus fossilized deictic material. Proto-Minitongue preserves LOC `-t` as the least specified member, INESS `-ti` for interior relation, and SUPER `-ta` for surface/support. The three endings are synchronically indivisible but remain analogically recognizable as a `t(V)` family.

PERL, INS, and COM descend from an older broad WITH/BY/THROUGH relation organized around `*m`. PERL and INS normally converge as `-m`, COM preserves `-ma`, and conservative PERL `-mi` remains productive with pronouns while surviving lexically on conventional route/path nouns. Route semantics historically favors retention or analogical extension of `-mi`, but the modern noun pattern is lexical: a merely contextual route construal does not create a new `-mi` form. The modern `m(V)` forms are indivisible case endings, but speakers recognize their relationship. Semantic and animacy-based specialization helps maintain the split: inanimate `-m` favors path, means, or functional association, while `-ma` favors companion/co-participant construal.

Ordinary nominal ABS is zero. Independent pronouns preserve an older, partly suppletive layer. Proto-Minitongue 1SG `na` and 2SG `si` are innovative strong stems; 1P inclusive `ma-` continues older comitative material, while 1P exclusive retains `na-`. The 2P and 3P nonsingular forms preserve old pronominal DU `*-i` and PL `*-a`. PERSON 3P `ri` continues an independent SOCIAL pronoun, while THING 3P `a` descends from a distal demonstrative. Core pronominal ERG `-ik`, GEN `-un`, and DAT `-ar` are relic endings of the older pronominal paradigm; ordinary sound change creates the modern syncretisms.

The modern pre-case /i/ linker is historically the ANIMATE class theme generalized beyond its original lexical class. The older noun-class system was semantically motivated and classifier-like, then became stem-integrated and partly opaque. An older SOCIAL class merged productively with MOBILE-LIVING to yield ANIMATE, while residual SOCIAL morphology survived separately in person-sensitive DAT and COM. Its fuller marker was `*ri`, cognate with the independent PERSON pronoun `ri`. Fusion with old DAT `*r` and COM `*ma` yielded modern SOCIAL DAT `-rir` and COM `-rima`; these fossil cells suppress the ordinary class theme/linker.

The surviving productive theme profiles are ANIMATE `*-i`, GROWING `*-a`, LAND/WATER `*-u`, and MADE `*Ø/C`. Semantic reassignment and sound change both contributed to the collapse of older classes, while high-frequency nouns retained fossil material from extinct distinctions. Apocope obscured class marking in many ABS.SG citation forms.

Analogy spread ANIMATE `*-i` as the default linker before consonantal cases, but conservative class pockets resisted leveling. GROWING retains `*-a` especially before LOC `*-t` and PERL/INS `*-m`, yielding transparent Proto-Minitongue `-at` and `-am`. LAND/WATER analogously preserves `*-u` in `-ut` and `-um`, with additional older `u ~ Ø` and consonantal relics surviving lexically. MADE was remodeled most strongly toward the /i/ default, though older artifact nouns may retain compact direct/fused `-m` PERL/INS, reinforced by frequent instrumental uses. ANIMATE's original class status is recoverable chiefly from irregular high-frequency fusions and from later number morphology rather than from a fully separate modern declension.

The first canonical lexical layer preserves a moderate, lexically distributed ABS.SG apocope. `nar` < *`nar-i`, `pir` < *`pir-i`, `tam` < *`tam-i`, `kir` < *`kir-i`, `mur` < *`mur-u`, and `rum` < *`rum-i` lose the final class theme word-finally, while suffixation protects the theme and therefore reveals it in inflected forms. Other bootstrap nouns retain their theme in the citation form. This gives six apocopated forms among sixteen eligible inherited theme-bearing nouns (37.5%), a moderate rather than categorical historical layer.

Three high-frequency route residues are now lexicalized. LAND/WATER `nuru` “river” developed a conventional route sense and retains conservative PERL `nurumi` beside ordinary INS `nurum`. MADE `ram` “boat” lexicalized a fused direct instrumental from older *`ram-m`, yielding INS `ram` syncretic with ABS, while the vowel-bearing conservative PERL remains `rammi`. LAND/WATER `ratu` “path; trail” lexicalized the MOVE stem *`ra` plus old PLACE `*-t` and a LAND/WATER theme; its conventional route use preserves PERL `ratumi` beside ordinary INS `ratum`. The `rammi` geminate is legal because `-mi` supplies the following nucleus; a word-final `*ramm` is not retained. A final small residue set resisted later leveling: LAND/WATER `tanu` and `karu` retain `u` before GEN (`tanun, karun`), while high-frequency ANIMATE `nar` and `pir` retain the old `i` before ESS (`narje, pirje`).

Mode-of-existence reassignment also created transparent lexical pairs: GROWING `pana` “tree” beside MADE `pan` “wood/timber,” LAND/WATER `kasu` “wild/natural fire” beside MADE `kas` “hearth/controlled fire,” GROWING `tana` “forest vegetation” beside LAND/WATER `tanu` “forest tract,” and GROWING `maka` “fruit; edible plant yield” beside MADE `mak` “prepared/portable food.” These pairs preserve the same semantic root while changing inherited class morphology or construal.

The settlement/architecture lexicon adds another layered place-to-artifact history. LAND/WATER `kinatu` “settlement; inhabited or formerly inhabited site” lexicalized SIT/SETTLE NONFINITE `kina` plus old PLACE `*-t` and LAND/WATER `*-u`, roughly “settling-place.” A younger folk association with `temis` “a bounded group of houses” reshaped how speakers interpret the word without replacing that older etymology. LAND/WATER `ratu` “path/trail” developed MADE `rat` “built passage”; archaic `ratam` “doorway/entrance” is a stored derivative of `rat` whose fossil material is no longer synchronically segmentable. Younger independent MADE `kis` then replaced `ratam` in ordinary speech. This is lexical replacement layered over class-shift history, not a productive doorway derivation.

Weather noun `suran` “rain” preserves the inherited root shared with `suru` “water” but has opaque old derivational material and ABS.SG apocope; speakers do not derive it productively from WATER. MADE `num` “roof” is an independent inherited architectural root. Ordinary repair uses biconsonantal `misa` “mend; patch; make serviceable.” In the place-personhood folktale, `nira` RETURN/RESTORE is deliberately used instead when the repaired structure is construed as being restored to a socially responsive place; this is a lexical-pragmatic contrast rather than a new repair category.

Dual `-e` and plural `-u` descend from class-independent number morphology. Contact with class themes creates class-sensitive surface outputs entirely through the regular vowel-contact and weight rules; no separate class-specific number allomorphs or analogical plural leveling are required. Regular sound change can therefore create number syncretism in case-marked forms.

A bounded GROUP/PORTION formative developed as nominal collective/paucal `-s`. It creates a derived bounded subset before ordinary number inflection and, by intervening between the lexical class theme and case, blocks the conservative class-theme case pockets; generalized `-i-` is used before consonantal case endings on these derived stems. A related light noun `sa` lexicalized from the same bounded-subset source and became the head of the GEN-based partitive `WHOLE-GEN + sa`. The two remain synchronically distinct: `-s` derives a nominal stem, while `sa` is a separate case-bearing PART head. PLACT/INT `-s` is another historical cognate of the same boundedness/grouping material.

A later attributive classifier layer developed partly from inherited class material and partly from newer semantic classifiers. PERSON `-ri` continues SOCIAL material; MOBILE-LIVING `-i`, GROWING `-a`, and FLUID/LANDSCAPE `-u` continue older class themes; COLLECTIVE `-s` continues bounded-group material. WORKED/MATERIAL `-t` and BOUNDED `-k` are later classifier innovations. The system remained sensitive to current construal and could cross-cut inherited declension, allowing, for example, a LAND/WATER noun to take PERSON classification in ritual discourse without changing its lexical case behavior.


### Possessive indexing

Inalienable possessive suffixes 1P `-i`, 2P `-a`, and 3P `-u` continue an ancient possessive-enclitic series independent of both the modern independent pronouns and verbal person consonants. The series grammaticalized after nominal number but before case, yielding the modern order `NOUN-(COLL/PCL)-NUMBER-POSS-CASE`. Its intervention leveled older noun-class case pockets and SOCIAL DAT/COM allomorphy in possessed forms. Alienable GEN possession remained a separate construction. Regular vowel contact and later coda shortening account for the accepted possessive-number syncretisms rather than special possessive allomorphy.


### Verbal relational and derivational morphology

The verbal system accumulated in layers rather than from one paradigm. REL `i-/Ø/a-` originated as weak relational preverbs and was generalized as INCREASE/MAINTAIN/DECREASE while remaining sensitive to lexical predicate classes and nominal case relations. HANDLE/TRANSFER `*k` conventionalized the directional triplet receive/take-toward-self ~ neutral transfer ~ give/send-away, while stative posture `*kn` conventionalized entry into ~ maintenance of ~ exit from a seated/settled posture. Reciprocal HANDLE/TRANSFER generalized only from the neutral MAINTAIN member: the two deictically anchored directional readings remained incompatible with one reciprocal event containing opposed transfer directions. KNOW/REMEMBER `*sn` similarly conventionalized INCREASE “come to know/realize” and DECREASE “forget/lose knowledge,” while SEARCH/TRACK `*sr` conventionalized INCREASE “find” and DECREASE “lose the trail.” RETURN/RESTORE `*nr` followed the opposite historical path: reversal/restoration became lexicalized in the root itself, so prefixed INCREASE/DECREASE forms failed to generalize. Causativized SEE/WITNESS `*se-m` conventionalized SHOW, after which REL extended within that derived predicate to REVEAL ~ SHOW ~ HIDE. ESS result complements likewise conventionalized with selected roots: INCREASE MOVE favored entry into landscape/route/role states, while INCREASE LIVE/GROW favored emergence as living or growing material. These lexical profiles reinforced the broader relational analysis without creating new REL morphology or a dedicated BECOME verb.

Pre-root participant indexing was reorganized as a uniform U series `n-k/t/p-`; the same person consonants survive post-root as A `-k/-t/-p`. Syntactic PO and U control consequently separated: applicative and voice constructions could alter PO while person-sensitive agreement continued to select a different U-controller.

VOICE represents several historical strata. REFL `r-` and MID `j-` descend from an older reflexive preverb *`ri-`. In frequent self-arising, anticausative, and internally caused predicates, the unstressed preverb underwent an early construction-specific reduction: *`ri-` > *`ji-` by palatal weakening of *r before *i, followed by loss of the weak high vowel, *`ji-` > *`j-`. This reduction began lexically in high-frequency predicates rather than as an automatic sound law. Once reduced *`j-` became associated with non-agentive/internal-arising semantics, analogy spread it to new middle predicates, while ordinary argumental reflexives restored/generalized the fuller `r-` shape from unreduced reflexive uses. The split therefore combines phonetic erosion with lexical diffusion and later morphological reanalysis; it predates the productive high-vowel syncope described below. RECP `s-` is inherited reciprocal morphology and is unrelated synchronically to post-root lexical `-s`. ANTIP `ma-` arose from frequent COM-marked object-demotion constructions: `P-COM ANTIP-V` favored case loss, pseudo-incorporation, and later lexical complex predicates.

AFF `-r` and GRD `-m` continue relational material also reflected in nominal DAT `-r` and INS/PERL `-m`. ORI `-p` grammaticalized separately from a TURN/FACE counterpart construction. This independent origin explains why the three applicatives form a semantic system without a single uniform historical source.

Lexical derivation precedes grade historically as well as synchronically. PLACT/INT `-s` descends from the boundedness/grouping source also reflected in nominal COLL/PCL `-s`. LEX.CAUS `-m` grammaticalized from MAKE/SHAPE and became stem-forming before grade; productive VAL.CAUS `-t` continues an older transitivizer outside grade. Frequent `PLACT/INT + MAKE` sequences conventionalized the productive inner-to-outer stack `ROOT-s-m`; the historically opposite `ROOT-m-s` sequence never generalized. One high-frequency pastoral residue survived: MOVE `*r` + LEX.CAUS `*-m` + PLACT `*-s` lexicalized as `r-m-s`, with regular repair yielding `rimisa` in the NF grade and the meaning “herd; drive a mobile group about.” Speakers store this as a lexical stem and do not infer a productive reverse-order derivation from it. Repeated derivation/valency combinations may otherwise lexicalize and later accept renewed productive morphology.

The inherited grade vowels were reanalyzed as a four-way predicate-status system: NF `-a`, REAL `-u`, IRREALIS `-i`, DEP `-e`. The old linking use of `-e` with auxiliaries is now understood as a dependent-grade construction. The lexical-vocalic root *`se` SEE/WITNESS still demonstrates regular grade alternation: *`se-a` > `sja`, *`se-u` > `sju`, *`se-i` > `së`, and *`se-e` > `sê`.

INST `-h` descends from a proximal/presentative particle *`hi` “here; this one here; here is this instance.” The particle first followed a graded predicate as a deictic presentative, selecting one salient manifestation from a more general property or event: schematically *`V-GRADE hi` “V, this instance here.” Frequent enclisis fixed it immediately outside grade; later applicative and valency material grammaticalized farther outside it. Reduction then gave *`-hi` > *`-h`, preserving the modern semantic center “salient particular manifestation/token” without acquiring aspectual or tense meaning. Its phonological erosion is advanced: before ordinary consonants it survives as strengthening (`hp/ht/hk/hm/hn/hs/hr > pp/tt/kk/mm/nn/ss/rr`), while protected `h` elsewhere triggers epenthetic /i/.

The core AUX inventory grammaticalized in two strata. Older phase predicates reduced to bound BEGIN `-ke`, CONTINUE `-je`, and FINISH `-te` and lost independent grade; CONTINUE later eroded further to IPFV `-j`. Younger modal constructions retained DEP forms of ordinary lexical predicates: KNOW/REMEMBER DEP `sine` > ABLE, MOVE/GO DEP `re` > INTEND, and GOOD/FIT DEP `sene` > NECESSARY. The inherited ordering tendency placed phase closest to the lexical predicate, followed outward by ABLE, INTEND, and NECESSARY. Reanalysis preserved two semantically useful reversible scope pairs, BEGIN↔ABLE and CONTINUE↔INTEND, while other inverse scopes remained periphrastic. LIVE/STAY followed a separate path: a fuller semi-productive customary/durative construction survives beside fully grammaticalized HAB `-r`. PFV `-n` is older and independent.

Predicate nominalization likewise combines independent strata. EVENT `-n` is inherited. STATE `-k` grammaticalized from an older HANDLE/HOLD/KEEP predicate *`ka`, the ancestor of modern HANDLE/TRANSFER `ka`. A postposed light-predicate construction *`[V-a] ka` “hold/keep the V-condition” was reanalyzed as a state/result nominal construction; semantic bleaching and loss of the final vowel yielded *`V-a-ka` > `V-a-k`. The HOLD/KEEP sense was subsequently lost as an independent modern sense while manipulation/transfer survived in the lexical verb. FACT `-p` descends more exactly from a lost distal/anaphoric demonstrative *`pa` “that one; that matter.” Clause-final *`pa` first resumed a preceding proposition (“that [event]”), then served as a complement-taking/clausal nominal head and finally bound as FACT `-p`. This source explains FACT's greater clause-likeness and its demonstrative > complementizer > fact-nominalizer trajectory. Older PARTICIPANT `-r` and RESULT/PLACE `-t ~ -m` survive only lexically or fossilized. Two creation-myth lexical items preserve this residue transparently without restoring productivity: `hat` “name” < SAY stem *`ha` + old RESULT `*-t`, and `kat` “gift; transferred offering” < HANDLE/TRANSFER stem *`ka` + old RESULT `*-t`; route noun `ratu` similarly preserves old PLACE `*-t`. Productive converbs conventionalized from EVENT+ESS, EVENT+INS, and EVENT+DAT; no productive ABL was added.

Two ordinary EVENT nominalizations also lexicalized semantically without changing the productive nominalizer. SLEEP `ma` + NONFINITE + EVENT gave `man` “dream; dream-event,” while SEE/WITNESS `se` + NONFINITE + EVENT gave `sjan` “vision; seen image.” The latter is only specifically oneiric when sleep or dream context supplies that interpretation; the transparent circumstantial construction `mane` “while sleeping” remains productive beside lexical `man`.

The old PARTICIPANT `*-r` survives one additional ritualized pocket without regaining productivity. Formal invocations preserve lexicalized `akar` “giver,” `kar` “keeper/holder,” and `rar` “walker/mover,” which underlie the ceremonial phrases `kumi akar`, `hat kar`, and `ratu rar`. Later fixed epithets `Kumakar`, `Hatkar`, and `Raturar` were lexicalized from these inherited title traditions; speakers do not use them as evidence for a productive compounding or participant-derivation rule.

### High-vowel syncope, final repair, and stress

A later rhythmic reduction favored heavier CVC and VCCV-like sequences without expanding the inherited onset inventory. Unstressed medial high vowels weakened, with epenthetic /i/ reducing most readily; /a e/ reductions remained older lexicalized residues. The change was constrained by the inherited `(C)(j)V(C)` syllable template, so deletion survived only where the whole output could be resyllabified with legal codas and onsets. This produced forms such as `nipitiruki → niptiruki` and `nipisjupi → nipsjupi`, while potential `*niptruki`, `*pant`, `*panast`, and `*nipknui` were blocked. The pattern remained productive in transparent morphology, while old lexical reductions became stored stems. Root vowels and inherited noun-class themes resisted the productive rule more strongly than repair vowels and generalized linking material.

The historical reduction was conditioned by the pre-reduction prosodic pattern, then stress was recalculated after reduction. Where several high vowels were eligible, reduction proceeded from right to left, with resyllabification after each successful deletion. Newly created closed syllables could therefore affect the final stress without retroactively protecting their vowel during the same reduction cycle.

A very small ritual residue escaped later leveling in memorized sacred formulas. Individual recitation forms may preserve a pre-syncope repair vowel, such as ritual `nipitiripi` beside ordinary `niptiripi` “will cut it.” These are lexicalized/formulaic archaisms transmitted with the sacred text, not evidence that speakers can freely suspend productive syncope.

After assimilation/fusion, resyllabification, and productive syncope, remaining illegal structures undergo restricted deletion and then /i/-epenthesis where protected lexical or morphological material would otherwise be lost. In multi-consonant morphological chains, repair proceeds from the rightmost problematic boundary leftward, inserting /i/ only as far left as necessary. Final stress is assigned only after these processes.

The resulting quantity-sensitive stress system selects the rightmost heavy syllable within the final three syllables; if that window contains no heavy syllable, stress is penultimate. All closed syllables are heavy, as are diphthongs and contracted bimoraic monophthongs.

The absolute chronology of changes that do not interact is not independently asserted; the relative ordering above is sufficient to derive the canonical Proto-Minitongue outputs.
